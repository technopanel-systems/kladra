TITLE: Bottom Sheets
CATEGORY: interaction
URL: https://designmotionhq.com/patterns/bottom-sheets

PROBLEM: On tall phones, menus anchored top-right sit in a dead zone the thumb can't reach one-handed.

RULES:
- Screens keep getting taller while thumbs stay the same length, turning the top of the display into a dead zone for one-handed use.
- Anchor menus and primary actions to the bottom of the screen (thumb's natural rest position), not the top-right corner most navs default to.
- Unlike a full modal that blocks everything, a bottom sheet keeps the underlying page visible so users never lose their place/context.
- Add snap points so the sheet can rest half-open (peek) or expand to full height, matching how much content is actually needed.
- Support drag-to-dismiss — a downward gesture matches the sheet's own direction and needs no small close target.
- Dim the background with a scrim and lock body scroll so only the sheet moves, keeping focus on the active task.

DO / DON'T:
- Do: place primary actions within thumb reach at the bottom of the screen.
- Do: keep the page visible behind the sheet to preserve context.
- Do: offer snap points and drag-to-dismiss for flexible, gesture-friendly height.
- Don't: bury frequent menus in the top-right dead zone on tall phones.
- Don't: block the whole screen with a full modal when a sheet would do.
- Don't: leave the background scrollable while the sheet is open.

KLADRA: Directly applicable to Kladra's phone-width layouts (375px is one of the required shot-looker widths) — any action sheet, filter panel, or quick-action menu on mobile should be a bottom sheet, not a dropdown pinned top-right. Record drawers themselves are probably closer to a side/bottom drawer already per DESIGN.md; this pattern argues the mobile variant of any drawer or action menu should open from the bottom with drag-to-dismiss, snap points, and a scrim. No conflict with drag-and-drop ban — drag-to-dismiss is a dismissal gesture on the sheet's own handle, not reordering/moving content, so it's a different mechanic; worth naming this distinction explicitly if it comes up in review. RTL: bottom sheets are vertical (no left/right concern), so no logical-CSS risk.

PRIORITY: medium — Kladra is Riyadh-based with reps in the field on phones, so mobile action reachability matters, but Kladra's drawer pattern is likely already bottom/side-anchored per DESIGN.md; this pattern mainly confirms the mobile variant and adds the drag-to-dismiss/snap-point vocabulary.
