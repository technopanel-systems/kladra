TITLE: Gradient Design
CATEGORY: visual
URL: https://designmotionhq.com/patterns/gradient-design

PROBLEM: A gradient can look premium or cheap purely based on how far it travels across the hue wheel, which direction lightness moves, and whether it sits under text.

RULES:
- Cheap-looking gradients usually travel too far across the hue wheel: neighboring hues (teal→cyan) blend cleanly; opposites (orange→blue) create a muddy gray dead zone in the middle. Video's specific number: stay within 60° of hue travel (video shows a color wheel with a 60° arc marked "Harmonious" vs a 180°-apart pairing marked "Muddy").
- Keep lightness moving in one direction — a gradient that gets darker, then lighter, then darker again reads as banding.
- Gradients work best as ambiance, not surface: a soft radial glow behind content beats a full-bleed linear wash on top of it.
- Subtle grain (2–3% noise) on top of a gradient hides banding on cheap displays and adds perceived texture.
- Direction matters: video's own 4-rule checklist names 135° as "The Premium Angle" specifically, shown against flatter 0°/45°/90° options.
- Mesh gradients (3 color points blended, not a simple 2-stop linear) read as more premium than a flat 2-color gradient.
- Never put body text directly in a gradient's mid-transition zone — contrast there is unpredictable; gradient text should be reserved for large, short headline-style text only, not body copy (video's own fail case: "Premium Design" gradient-filled headline flagged "UNREADABLE" when contrast drops mid-transition, vs body text kept solid-color "Text Only" next to a small gradient accent).

DO / DON'T:
- Do: stay within 60° of hue travel, add 2–3% noise, and test on a low-quality screen.
- Don't: put body text directly on a gradient's mid-transition zone — contrast is unpredictable there.

KLADRA: DIRECT CONFLICT with Kladra's standing rule "exactly two gradients (the primary action and the logo mark)." This pattern's own examples use gradients far more liberally than that — gradient buttons, gradient card headers/banners, gradient text, mesh-gradient backgrounds — all of which Kladra has explicitly ruled out beyond its two allowed spots. Say "conflicts with X" plainly: do not add mesh backgrounds, gradient text, or gradient card surfaces anywhere in Kladra. What IS transferable within the two allowed gradients: the 60°-hue-travel rule and the ~135° direction rule are good constraints for making Kladra's one primary-action gradient and one logo-mark gradient look premium rather than muddy, and the "never put body text in the mid-transition zone" rule is a real contrast risk if the primary-action button's label sits on the gradient. Apply the quality rules to the two permitted gradients only.

PRIORITY: medium — most of this pattern conflicts with Kladra's two-gradients cap and should be explicitly rejected as a general technique, but the hue-travel/direction/text-contrast rules are worth applying narrowly to keep Kladra's one primary-action gradient from looking muddy or the label unreadable.
